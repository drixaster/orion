The Solar System Structure

Explore our cosmic neighborhood categorized by stellar mass, solid worlds, and outer giants.

☀️ SUN

Sun

A yellow dwarf star sitting at the gravitational center of our solar system.

Contains 99.8% of all mass in the solar system and fuses roughly 600 million tons of hydrogen every second.

🗿 SOLID PLANETS

Mercury

The smallest terrestrial planet with extreme temperature swings.

Lacks a substantial atmosphere to trap heat, meaning temperatures drop drastically on its dark side despite proximity to the Sun.

Venus

Earth's scorching twin trapped in a runaway greenhouse state.

Surface pressures are heavy enough to crush steel, under an atmosphere composed almost entirely of carbon dioxide.

Earth

Our dynamic home world, uniquely balanced to support liquid surface water.

Protected by a powerful planetary magnetic field generated by fluid motion in its molten outer core.

Mars

The rusty red desert world targeted for future human exploration.

Home to Olympus Mons, a shield volcano nearly triple the height of Mount Everest.

🪐 OUTER GIANTS

Jupiter

The gas giant powerhouse containing dozens of documented moons.

Features the Great Red Spot, a massive storm larger than Earth that has raged for hundreds of years.

Saturn

The iconic ringed world composed primarily of water ice chunks.

Its majestic rings span hundreds of thousands of kilometers wide but are remarkably thin vertically.

Uranus

An ice giant that orbits the Sun on its side.

Thought to have been knocked askew by a massive collision early in its history, creating bizarre extreme seasonal changes.

Neptune

A distant world whipped by ferocious supersonic atmospheric winds.

Displays a deep azure coloration caused by atmospheric methane absorbing red light wavelengths.
